# WingMan — Fly together

Install WingMan.msi on Windows 10/11 x64. Start WingMan from the Start menu. It connects to MSFS automatically and retries every five seconds if the simulator is unavailable. WingMan finds a compatible SimConnect.dll automatically. If none is available, it downloads the unchanged SDK 1.7.3 runtime from the WingMan server and verifies its pinned SHA-256 before installing. It uses the SDK folder (normally `C:\MSFS 2024 SDK\SimConnect SDK\lib`); if that folder is protected or contains an incompatible DLL, it preserves the existing file and uses `%LOCALAPPDATA%\WingMan\MSFS 2024 SDK\SimConnect SDK\lib` instead. The selected path is stored in HKCU. Manual selection remains under More settings. No separate .NET installation is required.

The relay address is preset and hidden. **Collapse** switches to a compact window showing status, spacing and the Follow user switch; **Expand** restores the full view. Keep on top works in both views. Installation and Registry preferences are per-user and do not require administrator privileges on an unmanaged Windows PC. User settings are saved in HKCU, not JSON configuration files; legacy settings files are only read for migration.

1. Enter your pilot name once and click **OK** (or press Enter). WingMan connects to the relay automatically and reconnects after interruptions. The MSFS SDK does not supply the local Xbox gamertag; this is your chosen display name.
2. Meet wherever you like in MSFS. The list automatically shows nearby Followable WingMan pilots within 100 NM; no formation code is needed. WingMan pilots share their aircraft telemetry through the server. **Scan MSFS** adds simulator traffic and visible multiplayer labels to the same list; it does not stop an active follow. Local scanning continues every five seconds, with position polling and list refresh once per second.
3. Switch **Followable** on to let nearby pilots select you. Switches are green when on and red when off. Their names appear under your sharing section when they select you; the line stays blank otherwise.
4. Pale green rows are WingMan aircraft; light grey rows are MSFS-only contacts. Select an aircraft whose **Source / data** column says **Live**. Set **Behind**, **Right** and **Above** in 0.1 NM increments. Behind 0.0 with a nonzero Right offset flies alongside the lead. Negative offsets mean left/below; 0.1 NM vertically is about 608 ft. Row colour identifies the source, not telemetry freshness.
5. Enable the desired Speed, Heading and Altitude outputs and switch **Follow user** on. You must be airborne above 500 ft AGL, with fresh aircraft data and usable autopilot readback. You control AP master, speed/heading modes and autothrottle. PMDG 737 requires its SDK broadcast enabled and CMD engaged for automatic vertical matching. With **Automatic V/S for height matching** enabled, stock aircraft require AP master engaged and V/S or altitude hold selected. WingMan allows normal altitude capture and requests V/S again when another height correction is needed; an unconfirmed mode request stops following.

You can select a pilot while on the ground, but take off and climb to at least 500 ft above ground before switching **Follow user** on. WingMan does not taxi or take off for you. In version 1.1.1, trying to follow while stationary can show the misleading message “Invalid own-aircraft data”; this does not mean the chosen pilot's data is invalid.

Changing offsets or output checkboxes applies immediately without stopping a valid follow. Switching **Follow user** off (or pressing Escape) stops further autopilot commands and lets you select another pilot. The last settings remain in the aircraft. Sharing continues independently. You may lead and follow simultaneously, but circular follow chains are refused.

**Missing lead data (1.1.7):** an established WingMan follow continues from the last known ground course and speed, holding the last altitude. During a circle it holds the last centre and ground reference instead. Main and compact status show that data is remembered and how old it is; the aircraft list does not mislabel it as live. Fresh accepted telemetry automatically takes over, without switching Follow off. Predicted motion cannot account for unreported turns or speed changes. Memory is kept only during the running session and cannot start a new follow from a stale row.

**Auto-resume** handles interruptions that still stop control, such as losing your own simulator telemetry; it defaults to off and is stored in the Registry. With it enabled, the same lead resumes when fresh data and your aircraft are ready. Lead-only data loss uses remembered guidance regardless of this switch. Turning Follow off always cancels both. Explicit stops, aircraft/target/speed-mode changes, unsupported commands and a lead deliberately pausing/stopping sharing still require a new follow request.

**Circling a grounded lead:** set **Above NM** to a positive height and turn Follow user on. **Behind NM** becomes **Radius NM** and WingMan circles clockwise around the lead, following their location if they taxi. Your own aircraft must still be airborne at least 500 ft AGL. Height is relative to the lead's elevation; choose enough clearance for nearby terrain, because WingMan does not provide terrain avoidance. The Right offset is retained but disabled during the circle. **Circle IAS kt** appears while circling. Choose a fixed circling speed, including below the normal following minimum; configure flaps yourself. Max IAS/Mach still cap it. Until you choose a value, circle speed defaults to your following minimum plus ten knots. The choice is saved in the Registry, applies immediately, and does not change normal following speed limits. The calculated minimum circle radius decreases as your actual airspeed falls. A radius too small for current speed/wind widens automatically, with the actual radius shown in status and compact view. The size calculation assumes a nominal 20-degree turn; the aircraft's autopilot bank limit and response still affect the actual path. When the lead takes off, circling continues while the initial airborne samples establish motion, then the saved formation offsets apply automatically. Landing switches back to circling if Above NM is positive; otherwise following stops with an explanation. Setting Above NM to zero or negative during a circle also stops outputs. Missing lead data retains the last circle centre; own-telemetry, pause and command-confirmation checks remain active. This mode uses the lead's WingMan telemetry, not unreliable ground flags from the local multiplayer map.

**Circle below lead height:** in More settings, enable the checkbox and choose 500–20,000 ft AGL in 100 ft steps. When the lead is at or below this height, WingMan circles at that height above the terrain beneath the lead, temporarily ignoring Above NM. It returns to following once the lead is above the threshold plus 100 ft and airborne motion is established. This requires the lead to send AGL telemetry (WingMan 1.1.7 or newer); missing height is explained rather than guessed from MSL altitude. It is off by default. Grounded leads use their ground elevation, so they also work with the selected height. This is not terrain avoidance or automatic landing; your own aircraft must still remain at least 500 ft AGL.

Speed limits and climb/descent restrictions appear in the main following status, in orange while limiting control. **Minimum IAS**, **Maximum IAS** and the **Mach cap** are on the main screen. Your selected minimum is authoritative; the 1.3 × design-stall-speed reference is only a warning, allowing lower speeds with appropriate aircraft configuration. IAS and Mach are independent ceilings, not synchronized fields: their conversion depends on atmospheric pressure. The current effective cap is shown in both units; the lower ceiling applies. Raise the appropriate maximum to allow faster automatic catch-up without stopping Follow. More settings contains maximum V/S, smoothing, altitude circling, automatic V/S and updates. These are pilot-selected limits, not verified dynamic aircraft redlines; review them for your aircraft. Initial defaults use design stall/cruise speeds rather than airspeed during takeoff. Version 1.1.3 repairs the old automatically generated one-knot speed range when found in pre-1.1.3 preferences, without replacing unrelated custom limits. Climb commands remain held near the minimum speed until the aircraft accelerates.

Hover a pilot row for source details and aircraft information where available. Columns can be sorted by clicking their headings; refresh updates existing rows rather than rebuilding the list. **Name only** means a visible multiplayer label without navigable coordinates, so Follow user remains disabled for that row. **No position** and **Stale** contacts also cannot start following. Unnamed aircraft with usable coordinates appear by their source ID. Native MSFS contacts do not require the other pilot to run WingMan; availability depends on what the simulator exposes. Once MSFS supplies valid positions, those rows become followable automatically. Names/IDs from different sources are kept distinct to avoid selecting the wrong aircraft. Native APIs do not reliably distinguish AI from multiplayer aircraft. The simple list has no minimum-altitude filter. The local traffic map and detailed acquisition tools remain under More settings → Local traffic diagnostics.

Set Behind to **5 NM** and select the pilot immediately ahead to form an air train. Guidance uses a relative slot behind the lead's current course, not a recording of the lead's exact route. Aircraft performance, autopilot mode and configured speed limits determine whether the slot can be maintained. Review the More settings speed limits for your aircraft, especially when switching between jets and light aircraft. This is an experimental simulator add-on, with no automatic takeoff or landing.

Far behind, WingMan requests faster catch-up within your **Max IAS / Max Mach** limits. It gradually reduces the speed difference as it approaches the chosen spacing, accounting for current closing speed and aircraft response lag. For example, following at 4 NM from 100 NM away means closing 96 NM, then matching the lead's speed at the requested slot. The former fixed +40 kt catch-up cap was removed in 1.1.5. The speed ceilings and your aircraft's actual performance can still prevent catching a faster lead.

WingMan checks for updates **every five minutes**. More settings contains **Install updates automatically** and **Check for updates**. Downloads do not stop following. A verified update closes and restarts the app, restores the selected lead and offsets and automatically resumes only with fresh lead data and the same aircraft. A normal launch requires you to switch Follow user on again; own-telemetry interruptions resume only if **Auto-resume** was enabled; lead-only outages continue with remembered data. Changing your pilot name reconnects your identity and stops any current follow. Settings are stored under `HKCU\Software\WingMan`; updates and handoff files are in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\WingMan\updates`. Uninstalling the MSI preserves pilot preferences.

The bottom bar shows MSFS and relay connection states, the relay round-trip delay in milliseconds, and the app version. There are no manual connect/disconnect buttons.

Discovery is public: Followable broadcasts your name and aircraft position to nearby WingMan pilots and your selected followers. When Followable is off, your position still goes to the relay to find nearby pilots, but is not forwarded to other pilots. Names are supplied by users and are not verified Xbox identities.
